# Methodological audit

Audit basis: only `paper/01_repository_audit.md`, `paper/02_experiment_inventory.md`, `paper/03_result_registry.md`, and `paper/04_methodology.md`. No repository-wide rescan was performed.

## Overall verdict

The completed work supports a reproducible **descriptive, sample-relative LVI** and an **exploratory clustering analysis**. It does **not** currently support a prospective early-warning claim, external vulnerability prediction, causal claims about climate or socioeconomic drivers, or the headline Stage 3 claim that climate-only ML predicts vulnerability at approximately 80% accuracy/AUC 0.86. Those Stage 3 results are dominated by direct target construction and geographic leakage. The most defensible saved experiment is Stage 4's district-grouped, non-circular analysis; its best socioeconomic-target AUC is 0.6456 and accuracy is approximately the 0.6667 majority baseline.

Severity meanings:

- **CRITICAL:** invalidates a central predictive, causal, or early-warning claim.
- **MAJOR:** materially limits interpretation, generalization, or reproducibility.
- **MINOR:** should be corrected or disclosed but does not by itself overturn the analysis.
- **NO ISSUE:** the check is satisfactorily addressed within the documented scope.

## Exact required experiments and checks

### E0 — Merge-integrity audit

Create a read-only validation script that:

1. resolves the missing `hhid2` or assigns a documented immutable surrogate only for that row;
2. asserts unique keys in cleaned, LVI and clustered files;
3. performs one-to-one keyed joins, never positional assignment;
4. compares every shared column before/after join and aborts on mismatch; and
5. saves a join audit with matched, unmatched and duplicated counts.

### E1 — Nested spatial, non-circular ML validation

Implement a new script without overwriting existing results:

- Target: High tercile of the socioeconomic-only score, or preferably the external outcome from E2. Do not use any variable entering that target as a predictor.
- Features: the 22 exogenous climate/agricultural variables, with explicit household/plot/district labels. Exclude LVI components, flood variables if they enter the target, all PCs/clusters and numeric `district_id` as a continuous predictor.
- Groups: verified district/PSU; use reconstructed climate profiles only until official geography is obtained.
- Outer evaluation: 5-fold `StratifiedGroupKFold`, repeated across prespecified seeds if feasible; no household from an outer-test group may enter training.
- Inner selection: group-aware CV for hyperparameters, feature choices and threshold. All scaling, imputation and encoding must be fitted inside the inner training folds.
- Baselines: majority/prevalence, simple Logistic Regression, and closed-form/context-only baselines.
- Metrics: ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, accuracy, balanced accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, precision, F1, Brier, log loss, calibration intercept/slope, confusion matrix, and ordinal metrics where applicable.
- Uncertainty: 95% CIs from geographic-group bootstrap or fold distribution; report the 39-group effective sample.
- Artifacts: fold assignments, OOF probabilities/classes, parameters, thresholds, per-fold metrics and environment versions.

### E2 — External-outcome validation

Identify an outcome not used in LVI construction, such as subsequent food insecurity, consumption/income loss, adverse coping, poverty transition, crop loss or recovery failure. Prespecify its definition and timing. Test whether continuous LVI, LVI terciles and any ML model predict it under group-aware validation. Compare discrimination, calibration and incremental value over simple baselines. Without such an outcome, do not claim validated vulnerability prediction.

### E3 — Temporal/longitudinal forecasting

This cannot be resolved with the documented cross-section alone. Obtain another BIHS wave or dated outcomes and climate windows. Enforce:

- predictors measured strictly before the prediction origin;
- an explicit lead time;
- training on earlier periods and testing on later periods, with geographic blocking where possible; and
- no use of contemporaneous/future outcome components in predictors.

If those data are unavailable, remove the early-warning claim instead of simulating an experiment on the current data.

### E4 — Measurement-level feature ablation

Under the E1 outer folds, compare prespecified sets:

1. district climate/crop context only;
2. household flood variables only;
3. plot-level environmental variables only;
4. district + plot;
5. all non-circular exogenous variables.

Use identical folds and paired group-bootstrap differences in AUC/PR-AUC/Brier. This determines whether performance comes from household variation or repeated district context.

### E5 — Survey-design sensitivity

After obtaining weights, strata and PSUs, recompute weighted LVI distributions and tercile prevalence, cluster profiles, and key associations. Use design-based or PSU-bootstrap uncertainty. Compare weighted and unweighted conclusions. If weights cannot be recovered, explicitly restrict inference to the analyzed 5,605 households.

### E6 — Cluster-stability analysis

For k=2..8, repeat clustering under household and district/PSU bootstrap resamples. Refit standardization and PCA each time. Save silhouette distributions, cluster-size distributions, pairwise Jaccard/ARI stability and consensus assignments. Repeat after removing one redundant flood indicator and with Gower-distance PAM/hierarchical clustering. Retain a typology claim only if membership and interpretation are stable.

### E7 — LVI specification sensitivity

Recompute the complete LVI under a prespecified grid:

- verified literacy recode, current inferred illiteracy, and literacy omitted;
- loan as access, debt-burden alternative, and loan omitted;
- zero meals as observed, missing and excluded;
- both flood indicators versus one deduplicated flood indicator;
- equal-dimension versus equal-indicator weighting; and
- observed min-max versus fixed/theoretical and robust percentile bounds.

For every specification report score distributions, Spearman rank correlation, tertile agreement/Cohen's kappa, households changing High status, PCA/clustering stability, and downstream non-circular model sensitivity.
